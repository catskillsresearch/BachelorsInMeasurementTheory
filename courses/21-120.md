### (a) Differentiability and Derivative

Let $g(t) = \frac{\sin(t^2)}{1+t^2}$. Since $t \mapsto \sin(t^2)$ and $t \mapsto 1+t^2$ are continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and $1+t^2 \ge 1 > 0$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $g(t)$ is everywhere continuous. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:
$$f(x) = \int_0^x g(t)\,dt$$
is differentiable for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$, and:
$$f'(x) = g(x) = \frac{\sin(x^2)}{1+x^2}.$$

**Lean formalization.** The definition `f` is the indefinite integral $f(x)=\int_0^x \sin(t^2)/(1+t^2)\,dt$.

**Lemma** (`continuous_integrand`). The map $t\mapsto\sin(t^2)/(1+t^2)$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Proof.** Continuity of $\sin$ composed with $t\mapsto t^2$, divided by the nowhere-vanishing continuous map $t\mapsto 1+t^2$ (positivity of the denominator is discharged by `positivity`).

**Lemma** (`f_hasDerivAt`). For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $f$ has derivative $\sin(x^2)/(1+x^2)$ at $x$.

**Proof.** Apply Mathlib's interval-integral form of the fundamental theorem (`integral_hasStrictDerivAt`) to the continuous integrand, then weaken strict differentiability to `HasDerivAt`.

**Theorem** (`part_a_differentiable`). For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is differentiable at $x$.

**Proof.** Differentiability is the existence half of `f_hasDerivAt`.

**Theorem** (`part_a_deriv`). For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $f'(x)=\sin(x^2)/(1+x^2)$.

**Proof.** The derivative value is the `deriv` projection of `f_hasDerivAt`.
